## Prayer | Times, rak'ahs, conditions, how to pray
### Prayer and its times
<p>Prayer (salah) is the most important expression of servanthood to Allah. Five daily prayers are obligatory for every sane and mature Muslim.</p><ul><li><b>Fajr:</b> from true dawn until sunrise.</li><li><b>Dhuhr:</b> from when the sun passes its zenith until Asr.</li><li><b>Asr:</b> from the end of Dhuhr time until sunset.</li><li><b>Maghrib:</b> from sunset until the red twilight disappears.</li><li><b>Isha:</b> from the disappearance of twilight until imsak (dawn).</li></ul><p>Voluntary prayers are not performed at sunrise, when the sun is at its zenith, or when it reddens shortly before sunset.</p>
### How many rak'ahs are prayed?
<table class="ib-tab"><tr><th>Prayer</th><th>Sunnah</th><th>Fard</th><th>After</th></tr><tr><td>Fajr</td><td>2</td><td>2</td><td>—</td></tr><tr><td>Dhuhr</td><td>4</td><td>4</td><td>2 sunnah</td></tr><tr><td>Asr</td><td>4</td><td>4</td><td>—</td></tr><tr><td>Maghrib</td><td>—</td><td>3</td><td>2 sunnah</td></tr><tr><td>Isha</td><td>4</td><td>4</td><td>2 sunnah + 3 witr</td></tr></table><p class="note">The 2 of Fajr, the first 4 and last 2 of Dhuhr, the 2 of Maghrib and the last 2 of Isha are emphasised (mu'akkadah) sunnah. The first 4 of Asr and of Isha are non-emphasised sunnah. On Friday, the 2-rak'ah fard Friday prayer replaces Dhuhr; there are 4 rak'ahs of sunnah before it and 4 after.</p>
### Conditions of prayer
<p>There are six conditions that must be fulfilled before starting the prayer:</p><ol><li>Purity from ritual impurity: having wudu (and ghusl if required).</li><li>Purity from filth: body, clothing and place of prayer must be clean.</li><li>Covering the awrah.</li><li>Facing the qibla (the Kaaba).</li><li>Time: the prayer time must have begun.</li><li>Intention: knowing in one's heart which prayer one is praying.</li></ol>
### Obligatory acts of prayer
<p>There are six obligatory acts within the prayer; if one is missing, the prayer is not valid:</p><ol><li>The opening takbir</li><li>Standing (qiyam)</li><li>Recitation (qira'ah)</li><li>Bowing (ruku')</li><li>Prostration (sujud)</li><li>The final sitting (qa'dah akhirah)</li></ol>
### How to pray (example of 2 rak'ahs)
<ol><li>Face the qibla, make the intention and raise the hands to the ears saying "Allahu Akbar" (women to shoulder level).</li><li>Fold the hands (men below the navel, women on the chest). Recite Subhanaka, A'udhu-Bismillah, al-Fatiha and a surah or a few verses.</li><li>Bow into ruku' saying "Allahu Akbar" and say "Subhana rabbiyal azim" three times.</li><li>Rise saying "Sami'allahu liman hamidah", then say "Rabbana lakal hamd".</li><li>Go into sujud saying "Allahu Akbar" and say "Subhana rabbiyal a'la" three times. After a short sitting, make the second prostration.</li><li>Stand up and pray the second rak'ah in the same way (without Subhanaka and A'udhu; begin with Bismillah, al-Fatiha and a surah).</li><li>In the final sitting recite At-Tahiyyat, Allahumma salli, Allahumma barik and Rabbana atina.</li><li>Give salam first to the right, then to the left, saying "As-salamu alaykum wa rahmatullah".</li></ol><p class="note">It is recommended to learn the Arabic recitation of the prayer supplications from a teacher or a reliable book of fiqh.</p>
### What invalidates the prayer
<ul><li>Anything that invalidates wudu or ghusl.</li><li>Speaking, giving or returning salam.</li><li>Eating and drinking.</li><li>Turning (the chest) away from the qibla.</li><li>Laughing aloud.</li><li>Losing one of the conditions of prayer, exposing the awrah.</li><li>Misreading the Quran so badly that the meaning is corrupted.</li><li>Excessive movement unrelated to prayer that makes an onlooker think one is not praying (amal kathir).</li></ul>
### Congregation, Friday and travel
<p>Our Prophet (peace be upon him) said: <i>"Prayer in congregation is twenty-seven degrees superior to prayer offered alone."</i> (Bukhari, Muslim)</p><p>The Friday prayer is obligatory for men who are sane, mature, free, resident and healthy. On a journey of about 90 km or more (safar), the four-rak'ah fard prayers are prayed as two rak'ahs.</p>
### What are the wajib acts of prayer?
<ul><li>Reciting al-Fatiha followed by a surah or at least three short verses in the first two rak'ahs of fard prayers, and in every rak'ah of witr, sunnah and voluntary prayers.</li><li>Reciting al-Fatiha before the surah.</li><li>Performing ruku' and sujud without haste, letting every limb settle (ta'dil al-arkan).</li><li>Sitting after the second rak'ah in three- and four-rak'ah prayers (the first sitting).</li><li>Reciting At-Tahiyyat in the sittings.</li><li>Ending the prayer by saying "As-salamu alaykum wa rahmatullah".</li><li>Reciting the Qunut supplications in witr; saying the additional takbirs in the Eid prayers.</li><li>The imam reciting aloud in the first two rak'ahs of Fajr, Maghrib and Isha, and silently in Dhuhr and Asr.</li></ul><p class="note">If a wajib act is omitted by mistake, the prostration of forgetfulness is made; if it is omitted deliberately, the prayer must be repeated.</p>
### How is the prostration of forgetfulness (sahw) made?
<p>If a wajib act of the prayer is omitted or delayed by mistake, or a fard act is delayed, the prostration of forgetfulness is required.</p><ol><li>In the final sitting, recite only At-Tahiyyat.</li><li>Give salam to the right.</li><li>Make two prostrations saying "Allahu Akbar".</li><li>Sit again and recite At-Tahiyyat, Allahumma salli, Allahumma barik and the Rabbana supplications.</li><li>End the prayer by giving salam to both sides.</li></ol>
### How are missed (qada) prayers made up?
<p>Fard prayers and the witr prayer that could not be prayed on time are made up; sunnah prayers are not. Only if Fajr is made up before Dhuhr of the same day is it prayed together with its sunnah.</p><p>Qada prayers may be prayed at any time except the disliked times. Example intention: <i>"I intend, for the sake of Allah, to make up the fard of the first Fajr prayer I missed."</i></p><p class="note">To keep track of missed prayers you can use the qada counter in "Prayer tracking" on the home screen.</p>
### How is the Friday prayer performed?
<ol><li>When Dhuhr time begins, the first adhan is called and the 4-rak'ah first sunnah of Friday is prayed.</li><li>When the imam ascends the minbar, the second adhan is called and the sermon is listened to; one does not speak during the sermon.</li><li>The iqamah is called and the 2-rak'ah fard of Friday is prayed with the imam.</li><li>Then the 4-rak'ah last sunnah of Friday is prayed.</li></ol><p>Our Prophet (peace be upon him) recommended performing ghusl, wearing clean clothes and applying perfume on Friday.</p>
### The tarawih prayer
<p>An emphasised sunnah prayer of 20 rak'ahs prayed in Ramadan after Isha and before witr. Salam is usually given after every two or four rak'ahs. Praying it in congregation is sunnah; it may also be prayed alone.</p><p>Our Prophet (peace be upon him) said: <i>"Whoever spends the nights of Ramadan in worship out of faith and hoping for reward from Allah, his past sins will be forgiven."</i> (Bukhari, Muslim)</p>
## Wudu | Obligatory acts, sunnahs, what breaks it
### Obligatory acts of wudu
<p>According to Surah al-Ma'idah, verse 6, wudu has four obligatory acts:</p><ol><li>Washing the face</li><li>Washing the arms including the elbows</li><li>Wiping a quarter of the head</li><li>Washing the feet including the ankles</li></ol>
### How is wudu performed?
<ol><li>Make the intention and say "Bismillahir-rahmanir-rahim".</li><li>Wash the hands up to the wrists three times.</li><li>Take water into the mouth with the right palm and rinse three times (gargle if not fasting).</li><li>Sniff water into the nose and clean it three times.</li><li>Wash the face three times, from the hairline to below the chin and to the earlobes.</li><li>Wash the right arm, then the left, with the elbow, three times.</li><li>With wet hands wipe the whole head, then the ears and the neck.</li><li>Wash the right foot, then the left, with the ankles, three times.</li></ol>
### What breaks wudu
<ul><li>Anything exiting from the front or back passage (urine, faeces, wind, etc.).</li><li>Blood, pus or the like flowing from the body to a place that must be washed.</li><li>Vomiting a mouthful.</li><li>Sleeping (lying down or leaning), fainting, losing one's mind.</li><li>Laughing aloud during prayer.</li></ul>
### Sunnahs of wudu
<ul><li>Making the intention and beginning with Bismillah.</li><li>Washing the hands up to the wrists.</li><li>Using a miswak or brushing the teeth.</li><li>Rinsing the mouth and nose three times each.</li><li>Wiping the whole head, the ears and the neck.</li><li>Washing each limb three times.</li><li>Keeping the order (tartib) and doing it without interruption (muwalat).</li><li>Starting with the right; passing the fingers between the fingers and toes.</li></ul>
### Etiquette and disliked acts of wudu
<p><b>Etiquette:</b></p><ul><li>Performing it facing the qibla and sitting in a slightly raised place.</li><li>Not talking unnecessarily during wudu.</li><li>Saying the shahada and making du'a after wudu.</li></ul><p><b>Disliked acts:</b></p><ul><li>Wasting water; washing limbs more than three times.</li><li>Splashing water hard onto the face.</li><li>Omitting one of the sunnahs.</li></ul><p>Our Prophet (peace be upon him) advised not to waste water even beside a flowing river.</p>
### Situations that do not break wudu
<ul><li>Blood or pus from the body that stays in place and does not flow.</li><li>Vomiting less than a mouthful.</li><li>Dozing while sitting without leaning on anything.</li><li>Cutting nails or hair.</li><li>Touching one's spouse or a woman, according to the Hanafi school.</li><li>Laughing outside of prayer.</li></ul>
### Wiping over leather socks (khuff)
<p>Instead of washing the feet, one may wipe over waterproof leather socks that cover the feet including the ankles and were put on while in wudu.</p><ul><li>The period is 24 hours for a resident and 72 hours for a traveller.</li><li>The period begins from the first time wudu is broken after putting them on.</li><li>Wipe once over the top of the socks with three wet fingers, from the toes towards the leg.</li><li>The wiping becomes invalid when the period ends, the socks are removed or ghusl becomes necessary.</li></ul>
### Wiping over a bandage
<p>If washing or wiping a place bandaged because of a wound, fracture or surgery would cause harm, one wipes over the bandage in wudu and ghusl. Wiping most of the bandage once is enough. If the bandage falls off before the wound heals, the wiping is not invalidated.</p>
### Wudu of a person with an excuse
<p>If a condition such as urinary incontinence, continuous bleeding or constant passing of wind does not stop during an entire prayer time even for as long as it takes to make wudu and pray the fard, the person is considered "excused" (ma'dhur).</p><ul><li>The excused person makes one wudu for each prayer time and may pray as much as they wish with it during that time.</li><li>When the time ends, the wudu is broken.</li><li>If something other than their excuse breaks the wudu, they renew it.</li></ul>
### Acts of worship that require wudu
<ul><li>Praying (including the funeral prayer).</li><li>Making the prostration of recitation.</li><li>Circumambulating the Kaaba.</li><li>Touching the mushaf (the Holy Quran).</li></ul><p>A person without wudu may recite the Quran from memory and make du'a and dhikr.</p>
### The virtue of wudu
<p>Our Prophet (peace be upon him) said: <i>"Whoever makes wudu and does it well, his sins leave his body, even from beneath his nails."</i> (Muslim)</p><p>Wudu is the purification of both body and soul and a preparation for prayer. Staying in wudu and renewing it is a beautiful habit.</p>
## Ghusl and Tayammum | Full ablution and dry ablution
### Ghusl (full ablution)
<p>Ghusl has three obligatory acts:</p><ol><li>Rinsing the mouth</li><li>Rinsing the nose</li><li>Washing the entire body (leaving no dry spot)</li></ol><p>Ghusl is required after janabah, menstruation (hayd) and postnatal bleeding (nifas). Performing ghusl on Fridays and Eid days is sunnah.</p>
### Tayammum
<p>When no water can be found, or using water would cause harm due to illness or similar, tayammum with clean earth is performed instead of wudu and ghusl.</p><ol><li>Make the intention and say Bismillah.</li><li>Strike the hands on clean earth (or something of the earth) and wipe the face.</li><li>Strike the earth again and wipe the arms including the elbows.</li></ol><p class="note">Tayammum is invalidated when water is found.</p>
### How is ghusl performed?
<ol><li>Make the intention and say Bismillah.</li><li>Wash the hands and private parts; clean any impurity on the body.</li><li>Make wudu as for prayer (including rinsing the mouth and nose).</li><li>Pour water three times over the head, then the right shoulder, then the left shoulder.</li><li>Wash the whole body, rubbing it, making sure no spot stays dry (navel, behind the ears, roots of the hair).</li></ol>
### Situations requiring ghusl
<ul><li>Janabah (marital relations, or emission of semen with desire while asleep or awake).</li><li>The end of menstruation (hayd).</li><li>The end of postnatal bleeding (nifas).</li></ul><p>Also, washing a deceased Muslim (funeral ghusl) is a communal obligation.</p>
### What a person in janabah may not do
<ul><li>Pray.</li><li>Recite the Holy Quran or touch the mushaf.</li><li>Enter a mosque.</li><li>Circumambulate the Kaaba.</li></ul><p>They may make du'a and dhikr. It is not right to delay ghusl until the prayer time has passed.</p>
### Menstruation and postnatal bleeding
<p>According to the Hanafi school, menstruation lasts at least 3 and at most 10 days; postnatal bleeding at most 40 days.</p><ul><li>In these states one does not pray, and the missed prayers are not made up.</li><li>One does not fast; missed fasts are made up later.</li><li>One does not recite the Quran, touch the mushaf, enter a mosque or perform tawaf; du'a and dhikr are permitted.</li></ul>
### Do fillings, crowns and nail polish prevent ghusl?
<p>According to the Diyanet, dental fillings and crowns done out of necessity do not affect the validity of wudu and ghusl.</p><p>Layers that prevent water from reaching the skin, such as nail polish, must be removed before wudu and ghusl. Henna is not an obstacle since it lets water through.</p>
### Obligatory acts of tayammum
<ul><li>Making the intention.</li><li>Two strikes: striking the hands on the earth and wiping the face, then striking again and wiping the arms including the elbows.</li></ul><p>Tayammum is done the same way for wudu and ghusl; they differ only in the intention.</p>
### When is tayammum performed?
<ul><li>When there is no water within about 2 km.</li><li>When using water is feared to worsen an illness or delay recovery.</li><li>In severe cold when there is no means of heating water and there is a danger of falling ill.</li><li>When the water is only enough for drinking.</li><li>When reaching the water involves danger to life or property.</li></ul>
### What invalidates tayammum
<ul><li>Everything that invalidates wudu and ghusl also invalidates tayammum.</li><li>Finding water, or the end of the excuse preventing its use.</li></ul>
### Purity from filth (najasah)
<p>For prayer, the body, clothing and place of prayer must be free of najasah (impurities such as urine, faeces and blood).</p><ul><li>A place soiled by najasah is washed with clean water until the impurity and its trace are gone.</li><li>A place soiled by invisible najasah is washed three times, wringing it out each time.</li><li>If the najasah cannot be removed and the prayer time is about to end, the prayer is prayed as it is.</li></ul>
## Fasting | Ramadan fast, what breaks it, qada and fidyah
### What is fasting?
<p>Fasting is abstaining with intention from food, drink and marital relations from dawn (imsak) until sunset (the Maghrib adhan). Fasting in Ramadan is obligatory for every Muslim who is sane, mature, resident and healthy (al-Baqarah, 183-185). Women do not fast during menstruation and postnatal bleeding and make up the days later.</p>
### What breaks the fast and what does not
<p><b>Breaks it:</b></p><ul><li>Deliberately eating, drinking or smoking.</li><li>Marital relations.</li><li>Getting something into the stomach as medicine or food.</li><li>Deliberately vomiting a mouthful.</li></ul><p><b>Does not break it:</b></p><ul><li>Eating or drinking out of forgetfulness.</li><li>Vomiting involuntarily.</li><li>A wet dream during sleep.</li><li>Brushing the teeth (without swallowing), using a miswak.</li><li>Dust, smoke, a fly or the like entering the throat involuntarily.</li></ul>
### Qada, kaffarah and fidyah
<ul><li><b>Qada:</b> Making up, after Ramadan, as many days as were missed or broken for a valid reason.</li><li><b>Kaffarah:</b> One who breaks a Ramadan fast deliberately and without excuse by eating, drinking or marital relations fasts 60 consecutive days in addition to making up that day (if unable, feeds 60 poor people).</li><li><b>Fidyah:</b> Those unable to fast due to old age or an illness with no hope of recovery give, for each missed day, enough to feed one poor person. The current amount is announced each year by religious authorities.</li></ul>
### Suhur, iftar and voluntary fasts
<p>Our Prophet (peace be upon him) said: <i>"Eat suhur, for in suhur there is blessing."</i> (Bukhari, Muslim) Iftar is hastened as soon as its time begins.</p><ul><li>Mondays and Thursdays</li><li>The day of Ashura (the 10th of Muharram; together with the 9th or 11th)</li><li>6 days in Shawwal</li><li>The 13th, 14th and 15th of each month (the white days)</li></ul><p class="note">Fasting on the first day of Eid al-Fitr and the four days of Eid al-Adha is forbidden.</p>
### When is the intention for fasting made?
<p>For the Ramadan fast, certain voluntary fasts and specified vows, the intention can be made from the evening until the forenoon of the next day (before the midpoint between imsak and sunset).</p><p>For qada and kaffarah fasts and unspecified vows, the intention must be made before imsak. The intention is made in the heart; getting up for suhur also counts as intention.</p>
### Excuses that permit not fasting
<ul><li>Illness, or fear that fasting will worsen an illness.</li><li>Travel (a journey of about 90 km or more).</li><li>Pregnancy and breastfeeding (fear of harm to mother or baby).</li><li>Old age.</li><li>Hunger and thirst that endanger life; coercion.</li></ul><p class="note">Fasts missed for an excuse are made up later; the chronically ill with no hope of recovery and the elderly give fidyah.</p>
### Do injections, medicine or blood tests break the fast?
<ul><li>Non-nutritive therapeutic injections and vaccines do not break the fast.</li><li>Having blood taken or donating blood does not break the fast.</li><li>Eye drops do not break the fast.</li><li>Brushing the teeth without swallowing does not break the fast.</li><li>Medicine taken by mouth and nutritive drips break the fast.</li></ul><p class="note">For special cases such as asthma inhalers or nasal sprays, consult a qualified scholar.</p>
### Days on which fasting is forbidden or disliked
<p>Fasting on the first day of Eid al-Fitr and the four days of Eid al-Adha is forbidden.</p><p>Singling out only Friday or only Saturday for fasting, and fasting continuously without a break (sawm ad-dahr), is disliked.</p>
### What is i'tikaf?
<p>I'tikaf is staying in a mosque for a period with the intention of worship. I'tikaf in the last ten days of Ramadan is an emphasised communal sunnah; our Prophet (peace be upon him) observed i'tikaf every year in the last ten days of Ramadan (Bukhari, Muslim).</p><p>The person in i'tikaf does not leave the mosque except for necessary needs and spends the time in prayer, Quran, dhikr and du'a.</p>
### The virtue of Ramadan and fasting
<p>Our Prophet (peace be upon him) said: <i>"Whoever fasts Ramadan out of faith and hoping for reward from Allah, his past sins will be forgiven."</i> (Bukhari, Muslim)</p><p><i>"Fasting is a shield. Let the fasting person not speak obscenely or shout."</i> (Bukhari, Muslim)</p><p>Fasting teaches patience, gratitude, understanding of the poor and mastery over oneself.</p>
## Zakat | Nisab, rate, recipients, sadaqat al-fitr
### For whom is zakat obligatory?
<p>Zakat is a financial act of worship. It is obligatory for a person who meets these conditions:</p><ol><li>Being Muslim, sane and mature.</li><li>Owning wealth reaching the nisab beyond basic needs and debts.</li><li>One lunar year having passed over this wealth.</li></ol>
### Nisab and rate
<p>The nisab is the value of 80.18 grams of gold or 561 grams of silver. The rate of zakat is one fortieth, i.e. <b>2.5%</b>. Gold, silver, money, trade goods and livestock above certain numbers are subject to zakat. On agricultural produce, ushr is given at harvest (one tenth if rain-watered, one twentieth if irrigated by labour).</p><p class="note">Since gold and silver prices change daily, calculate the nisab with current prices.</p>
### To whom is zakat given?
<p>According to Surah at-Tawbah, verse 60, zakat is given to eight groups:</p><ol><li>The poor</li><li>The needy</li><li>Zakat collectors</li><li>Those whose hearts are to be reconciled to Islam</li><li>Slaves (to free them)</li><li>Debtors</li><li>Those in the cause of Allah (knowledge, service, etc.)</li><li>Stranded travellers</li></ol><p><b>Not to be given to:</b> one's parents and grandparents, children and grandchildren, spouse; the wealthy who own the nisab; non-Muslims.</p>
### Sadaqat al-fitr
<p>A person who owns wealth reaching the nisab beyond basic needs gives sadaqat al-fitr for themselves and those they are responsible for. It is preferably given before the Eid al-Fitr prayer. The amount is announced each year by religious authorities.</p>
### On which kinds of wealth is zakat given?
<ul><li>Gold, silver and jewellery.</li><li>Money: cash, bank accounts, foreign currency, receivables.</li><li>Trade goods (goods bought for sale, stock).</li><li>Sheep, goats, cattle and camels reaching certain numbers (sheep-goats 40, cattle 30, camels 5).</li><li>Agricultural produce (as ushr, at harvest).</li></ul><p>Basic needs such as the house one lives in, the car one drives, household goods and work tools are not subject to zakat.</p>
### How is zakat calculated?
<ol><li>On the lunar (Hijri) anniversary of the day you reached the nisab, add up all your zakatable assets.</li><li>Deduct your debts from them.</li><li>If the remainder exceeds the value of the nisab (80.18 g of gold), give one fortieth (2.5%) as zakat.</li></ol><p><b>Example:</b> If after deducting debts you have assets worth 400,000, your zakat is 400,000 × 0.025 = <b>10,000</b>.</p>
### When is zakat given?
<p>When a lunar year has passed over wealth reaching the nisab, zakat becomes due and should be given without delay. It need not be given in Ramadan, though it is preferred in hope of greater reward. Paying in advance before the year ends is also permissible.</p>
### The intention and giving of zakat
<p>Zakat is given with the intention made at the time of giving or when setting aside the wealth for zakat. It is not necessary to tell the recipient that it is zakat. Zakat must pass into the ownership of the recipient; therefore it is not given to services that do not become someone's property, such as building a mosque (sadaqah may be given).</p>
### Is a woman's jewellery subject to zakat?
<p>According to the Hanafi school, gold and silver are subject to zakat, including jewellery that is worn. If a woman's jewellery and other assets reach the nisab and a year passes over them, she gives its zakat.</p>
### Sadaqah and continuous charity
<p>Sadaqah is any good deed done sincerely for Allah's pleasure; unlike zakat it has no fixed amount. A smile, a kind word and removing an obstacle from the road are also sadaqah.</p><p>Our Prophet (peace be upon him) said: <i>"When a person dies, his deeds come to an end except for three: continuous charity, beneficial knowledge and a righteous child who prays for him."</i> (Muslim) Lasting good works such as fountains, schools, mosques and trees are continuous charity.</p>
## Hajj and Umrah | Conditions, obligatory and wajib acts, Umrah
### For whom is Hajj obligatory?
<p>Hajj is obligatory once in a lifetime for those who are able (Al 'Imran, 97). Its conditions:</p><ol><li>Being Muslim, sane and mature.</li><li>Having health and physical ability.</li><li>Having the financial means to cover the journey there and back and the maintenance of those one leaves behind.</li><li>The route being safe.</li><li>For women, being accompanied by a husband or mahram, or a trustworthy Hajj group.</li></ol>
### Obligatory and wajib acts of Hajj
<p><b>Obligatory:</b> Entering ihram, standing at Arafat (9th of Dhu al-Hijjah), the tawaf of visitation.</p><p><b>Wajib:</b> Standing at Muzdalifah, sa'y between Safa and Marwa (7 rounds), stoning the jamarat, shaving or shortening the hair, the farewell tawaf, and the thanksgiving sacrifice for those performing tamattu' and qiran.</p><p>The days of Hajj are the 8th to 13th of Dhu al-Hijjah.</p>
### Prohibitions of ihram
<ul><li>Marital relations and what leads to them.</li><li>Applying perfume.</li><li>Cutting hair, beard or nails.</li><li>Hunting land animals, picking plants.</li><li>Men wearing stitched clothing or covering the head; women covering the face.</li><li>Quarrelling and foul speech.</li></ul>
### Umrah
<p>Umrah is an act of worship related to the Kaaba and Mecca like Hajj, which can be performed on any day of the year, and is sunnah. How it is done: enter ihram at the miqat, circumambulate the Kaaba seven times, perform sa'y between Safa and Marwa and shave or shorten the hair.</p>
### The miqat points
<p>These are the boundary points that those coming to Mecca for Hajj or Umrah may not pass without entering ihram:</p><ul><li>Dhul-Hulayfah (from Medina)</li><li>Al-Juhfah (from Syria, Egypt and Turkey; today Rabigh)</li><li>Qarn al-Manazil (from Najd)</li><li>Yalamlam (from Yemen)</li><li>Dhat Irq (from Iraq)</li></ul>
### Types of Hajj
<ul><li><b>Ifrad:</b> Ihram is entered for Hajj only.</li><li><b>Tamattu':</b> During the Hajj months Umrah is performed first and ihram is left, then ihram is entered again for Hajj. The thanksgiving sacrifice is wajib.</li><li><b>Qiran:</b> Hajj and Umrah are intended together with a single ihram. The thanksgiving sacrifice is wajib.</li></ul><p>Most pilgrims from Turkey perform tamattu'.</p>
### How is tawaf performed?
<ol><li>Be in wudu; start in line with the Black Stone.</li><li>Circle the Kaaba seven times (shawt), keeping it on your left.</li><li>Greet the Black Stone in each round (istilam).</li><li>After the tawaf, if possible, pray two rak'ahs behind Maqam Ibrahim.</li><li>Drink Zamzam and make du'a.</li></ol>
### What is done on the days of Hajj?
<ul><li><b>8 Dhu al-Hijjah (Tarwiyah):</b> Pilgrims go to Mina.</li><li><b>9 Dhu al-Hijjah (Arafah):</b> The standing at Arafat is performed in the afternoon; at night pilgrims move to Muzdalifah.</li><li><b>10 Dhu al-Hijjah (1st day of Eid):</b> After Muzdalifah, the Aqabah pillar is stoned, the sacrifice is made, the hair is shaved and the tawaf of visitation is performed.</li><li><b>11-12 (13) Dhu al-Hijjah:</b> Pilgrims stay in Mina and stone the three pillars each day.</li><li>The farewell tawaf is performed on leaving Mecca.</li></ul>
### Visiting Medina
<p>Visiting Medina is not part of Hajj, but visiting the mosque and grave of our Prophet (peace be upon him) is very virtuous. One prays in the Prophet's Mosque, makes du'a in the Rawdah, sends greetings to our Prophet and his two caliphs, and also visits Quba Mosque and the martyrs of Uhud.</p>
### The virtue of Hajj
<p>Our Prophet (peace be upon him) said: <i>"Whoever performs Hajj for Allah and does not speak obscenely or commit sin, returns (sinless) as on the day his mother bore him."</i> (Bukhari, Muslim)</p><p><i>"The reward of an accepted Hajj is nothing but Paradise."</i> (Bukhari, Muslim)</p>
## Sacrifice (Qurban) | Who must offer it, which animal, how
### For whom is the sacrifice wajib?
<p>Offering a sacrifice during the days of Eid is wajib for a Muslim who is resident and owns wealth reaching the nisab. The sacrifice is an animal slaughtered with the intention of drawing near to Allah and mentioning His name.</p>
### Time and animals
<p>It is offered on the 1st, 2nd and 3rd days of Eid al-Adha (10-12 Dhu al-Hijjah). It is not offered before the Eid prayer.</p><table class="ib-tab"><tr><th>Animal</th><th>Minimum age</th><th>Sharing</th></tr><tr><td>Sheep, goat</td><td>1 year (a sheep of 6 months if it looks one year old)</td><td>1 person</td></tr><tr><td>Cattle, buffalo</td><td>2 years</td><td>up to 7 people</td></tr><tr><td>Camel</td><td>5 years</td><td>up to 7 people</td></tr></table><p class="note">An animal with obvious defects in the eye, leg or ear, or that is emaciated or sick, is not sacrificed.</p>
### How is it slaughtered and distributed?
<p>It is slaughtered without causing the animal suffering, without sharpening the knife in front of it, saying "Bismillahi Allahu Akbar". Dividing the meat into three (part for the family, part for relatives and neighbours, part for the poor) is recommended. The butcher's fee is not paid from the meat of the sacrifice.</p>
### The wisdom of the sacrifice
<p>The sacrifice is an act of worship to draw near to Allah and thank Him for His blessings. The submission shown by Ibrahim in the test of sacrificing his son Isma'il, and Allah sending them a ram as ransom (as-Saffat, 102-107), is the memory behind this worship.</p><p><i>"Neither their meat nor their blood reaches Allah; what reaches Him is your piety."</i> (al-Hajj, 37)</p>
### What is said during the slaughter?
<p>The animal is laid facing the qibla and slaughtered saying <i>"Bismillahi Allahu Akbar"</i>. Before slaughtering, this verse may be recited: <i>"Indeed, my prayer, my worship, my life and my death are for Allah, Lord of the worlds."</i> (al-An'am, 162)</p><p>The slaughter is done without causing suffering, with a sharp knife and out of sight of other animals.</p>
### Sacrifice by proxy
<p>A person may slaughter the sacrifice himself or appoint a trusted person or a charitable organisation as proxy. The proxy may be given verbally, in writing or by phone. The slaughter must be done in the name of the one who appointed the proxy and during the days of Eid.</p>
### Points to observe when sharing
<ul><li>Up to seven people may share a large animal such as cattle or a camel.</li><li>All partners must join with the intention of worship; if one joins only for meat, the others' sacrifice is not valid either.</li><li>The meat is weighed and divided equally.</li></ul>
### Vowed sacrifice and aqiqah
<p><b>Vowed sacrifice:</b> Vowing to offer a sacrifice if something comes to pass; when it does, offering it becomes wajib. The one who made the vow and those they support may not eat its meat; all of it is given to the poor.</p><p><b>Aqiqah:</b> A sacrifice offered in gratitude for the birth of a child; it is recommended, preferably on the seventh day after birth.</p>
### Can the skin and meat of the sacrifice be sold?
<p>The meat, skin and other parts of the sacrifice cannot be sold or given to the butcher as payment. The skin may be used by the person himself or donated to a charity. If it is sold, its price must be given as sadaqah.</p>
### What if one could not offer the sacrifice in time?
<p>A person for whom the sacrifice was wajib but who did not offer it during the days of Eid gives the value of a sacrificial sheep to the poor as sadaqah after Eid. If an animal was bought but not slaughtered, the animal is given alive as sadaqah.</p>
### Takbir at-tashriq during Eid al-Adha
<p>From the Fajr prayer on the Day of Arafah until the Asr prayer on the fourth day of Eid, saying the takbir at-tashriq after a total of 23 fard prayers is wajib:</p><p style="text-align:center"><i>"Allahu akbar Allahu akbar, la ilaha illallahu wallahu akbar, Allahu akbar wa lillahil-hamd."</i></p>
